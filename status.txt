ARROW-GLYPH done: the header now uses the glyphs the user specified, U+FE40 / U+FE3F (presentation-form vertical angle brackets). This entry records a measurement failure of mine, because that is the part worth remembering. The user asked for an angle-bracket-like chevron, obtuse, about 120 degrees. My first probe computed acos((k*k-1)/(k*k+1)); the correct expression is acos((1-k*k)/(1+k*k)), so every reading was mirrored to 180-true. Two wrong conclusions followed: (a) I asserted the existing filled triangles were already at the requested angle (118.2/121.6) and that the complaint was only about weight -- they are in fact 62.0/58.9 degrees, ACUTE; (b) on that basis I swapped in U+2228/U+2227, reporting 127.7/131.4 degrees -- they are in fact 53.1/49.1 degrees, also ACUTE. So I answered a request for an OBTUSE chevron with an ACUTE one, twice, off the same broken instrument. The corrected probe CALIBRATES FIRST: it rasterises synthetic chevrons of analytically known angle (60/90/110/120/130/150) and asserts the measurement recovers them (error <= 1.4 deg) before touching a real glyph. Corrected readings: the user's glyphs measure 134.5 and 135.7 degrees, thin-stroked, and only 1.2 degrees apart (more symmetric than the 3.7 of my wrong pair). LESSON: a measurement function is an instrument, and an uncalibrated instrument produces confident nonsense -- prove the instrument before drawing conclusions from it. A second, smaller self-inflicted error is also recorded: I hand-wrote the UTF-8 bytes for U+FE40 as EF B8 80 when they are EF B9 80 (EF B8 80 is U+FE00), so multi-byte literals are now generated programmatically via chr(cp).encode('utf-8') and the bytes are written into the comment. Glyph availability was likewise measured rather than assumed: U+2304, U+2BC6 and U+2329 are MISSING from the current font, and a missing glyph gets silently font-linked to another face, which throws off weight and baseline and looks worse than the original.
